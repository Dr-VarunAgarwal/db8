Principles of Epistemic Debate & Behavior

• 6 Core Standards (Click to expand)
▾
1. Principle of Charity

Interpret your partner's statement in its strongest, most rational form before critiquing.

2. Rapoport's Rules

Re-express your partner's position clearly enough that they confirm it before offering rebuttal.

3. Hitchens's Razor

What can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence.

4. Hanlon's Razor

Never attribute to malice what can be adequately explained by differing premises or oversight.

5. Falsifiability

State what evidence or boundary condition would cause you to revise your stance.

6. Is vs. Ought Guillotine

Separate empirical facts ("what is") from moral value judgments ("what ought to be").

How Crux Works

1. Private Pre-Flight Audit (Before Posting)

Before your argument reaches your partner, the AI privately surfaces unstated assumptions, broad generalizations, and potential cognitive biases—giving you the chance to refine your boundary conditions first.

2. Mandatory Comprehension Gate

You cannot reply to an argument until you accept the AI's distillation of your partner's claim or write a 1-sentence steel-man verified as accurate.

3. Socratic Referee, Never Ghostwriter

Crux never writes arguments for you and never declares a winner on moral questions. It separates empirical facts from value conflicts and maps the exact root crux.

4. Anti-Tribal Co-Reasoning & Stance Versioning

Instead of upvotes or dunking, theses evolve visibly (v1.0 → v1.1) when a partner exposes a blind spot, earning Δ clarification pins.
